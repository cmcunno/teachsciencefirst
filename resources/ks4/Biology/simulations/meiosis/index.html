The Complete Lifecycle Cycle

1. Gametes Join (Fertilisation)
A haploid sperm nucleus ($n=23$) fuses with a haploid egg nucleus ($n=23$). This restores the full diploid chromosome number ($2n=46$).
2. Zygote Divides by Mitosis
The single-celled zygote begins rapid cell division via mitosis (1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 → ball of cells). Each new cell has an identical copy of all 46 chromosomes.
3. Differentiation into Embryo
As the embryo develops, cells differentiate (specialize) to form distinct tissues and organs: nerve cells, muscle cells, heart, and limbs.

Feature Meiosis Mitosis AQA Exam Tip
Purpose & Primary Role
Production of gametes (sperm & egg in humans; pollen & ovule in plants) for sexual reproduction.
Growth, repair of damaged tissues, replacement of worn-out cells, and asexual reproduction.
Always state "repair of tissue" or "replacement of cells", never "repairing cells" (cells are replaced).
Where it Occurs (Location)
Reproductive organs only (human testes & ovaries; flowering plant anthers & ovaries).
All ordinary body (somatic) cells throughout the entire organism.
AQA specification: "Cells in reproductive organs divide by meiosis to form gametes".
Number of Cell Divisions
2 divisions (Division 1 separates homologous pairs; Division 2 separates chromatids).
1 division (Single separation of sister chromatids).
Remember: 2 divisions halve the chromosome count to form 4 cells.
Number of Daughter Cells
4 daughter cells (gametes).
2 daughter cells.
One parent cell produces 4 sperm or egg cells in meiosis; 2 identical cells in mitosis.
Chromosome Number
Halved (Haploid, n = 23 in humans) — contains one chromosome from each homologous pair.
Preserved / Same (Diploid, 2n = 46 in humans) — full double set of chromosomes.
Halved so when two gametes fuse during fertilisation, the normal diploid number (46) is restored in the zygote.
Genetic Variation / Identity
All 4 cells are genetically different from each other and different from the parent cell.
Cells are genetically identical clones to each other and to the parent cell.
Meiosis introduces variation essential for natural selection and species survival in changing environments.
Type of Reproduction
Associated with sexual reproduction (involves fusion of two gametes).
Associated with asexual reproduction (single parent, no fusion of gametes).
In asexual reproduction, there is no mixing of genetic material so offspring are identical clones.

Subject Content:

Students should be able to explain how meiosis halves the number of chromosomes in gametes and fertilisation restores the full number of chromosomes.

Cells in reproductive organs divide by meiosis to form gametes.

When a cell divides to form gametes:

  • copies of the genetic information are made
  • the cell divides twice to form four gametes, each with a single set of chromosomes
  • all gametes are genetically different from each other.

Gametes join at fertilisation to restore the normal number of chromosomes. The new cell divides by mitosis. The number of cells increases. As the embryo develops cells differentiate.

Exam Specification Directive: Knowledge of the stages of meiosis is not required.
